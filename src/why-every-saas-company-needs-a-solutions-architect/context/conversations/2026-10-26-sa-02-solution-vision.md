# SA-02 — Delegated activities, solution vision and accountability — 2026-10-26

Record of the article-related SA-02 discussion supplied in this session. Author excerpts and English proposals below are verbatim; connecting explanations are summaries. Repeated readings and operational acknowledgments are omitted. This is not a complete transcript of every message or tool action.

## Starting point — summary

After SA-01, the author requested the next improvement. The assistant proposed retaining the existing sentence “This process cannot be reliably split across multiple roles.” and immediately explaining the distinction between delegated activities and unified vision/accountability.

The existing paragraph in [article.md](../../article.md) was:

> This process cannot be reliably split across multiple roles. When it is fragmented, every group protects its own priority, and nobody owns the balance.

## First proposed insertion — verbatim

> The activities can be delegated, but the architectural vision and final accountability must remain with one person.

The author requested reading the insertion together with the preceding and following sentences.

## Author refinement: one mind and responsibility — verbatim

> Ага, смотри, всё в принципе неплохо, но давай как бы подумаем с такой точки зрения. Э ... да, активности могут быть делегированы, но видение оно может быть только в одном уме. И соответствующее ответственность за принятые решения. Как-то так вот я думаю

The assistant proposed:

> Activities can be delegated, but the architectural vision must live in one mind, with corresponding accountability for decisions.

## Author refinement: broader solution vision — verbatim

> Слушай, а нам точно нужен именно architectural vision? А почему бы не оставить просто vision? Ну, моя точка зрения о том, что как бы вот этот человек, он в принципе ещё должен быть визионером в некотором смысле

The assistant offered “the solution vision” or “the vision for the whole solution” to preserve the connection to the whole solution while avoiding a narrowly architectural reading.

The author chose:

> Давай вот vision solution. Прочитай еще раз себе, еще раз

The subsequent reading used this proposed insertion:

> Activities can be delegated, but the solution vision must live in one mind, with corresponding accountability for decisions.

## Author refinement: “can only” rather than “must” — verbatim

> Слушай, а вот точно ли нам надо must live in one mind? Может быть can live так мы как-то вроде... ну если мы must говорим, мы типа ну так вот так должно быть, а почему хрен его знает. А когда мы используем can, ну тут скорее звучит как типа ну бля, ребята, реальность такая, короче, ну видение может быть только у одного

The author's distinction was descriptive versus prescriptive: the intended thesis is that the vision can only live in one mind, rather than an unexplained instruction that it must. The assistant proposed “can only” to express the intended restriction; “can” alone would not express “only.”

## Final proposed insertion — verbatim

> Activities can be delegated, but the solution vision can only live in one mind, with corresponding accountability for decisions.

## Author approval — verbatim

> Да, так и оставляем
